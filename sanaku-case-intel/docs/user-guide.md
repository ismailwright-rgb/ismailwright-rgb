# User guide

This guide covers everything you'll see and use in your firm's
case-research tool. The name, logo, and colors shown in your copy are
your firm's own — set for you when it was installed — so what you see on
screen may not match every detail described here, but every feature
below works the same way regardless of how it's branded.

## What this tool does

You ask a question about a case, in plain English, and it answers using
only that case's own documents — every fact in the answer is followed by
exactly where it came from: the document name and the page number. You
can click through to read the original passage for yourself. Nothing is
ever stated without a source you can check.

It never guesses beyond what's actually in the case's documents. If the
documents don't say something, the answer will say so rather than
filling in a plausible-sounding guess.

## Getting started

1. Open the tool in your browser, using the address you were given when
   it was set up.
2. **Pick a case.** If your firm has more than one case loaded, you'll
   see a dropdown list — choose the one you're working on. If there's
   only one case loaded, its name is already filled in for you.
3. **Type your question** into the question box and press **Ask** (or
   hit Enter).

That's the whole core workflow. Everything else below is either a
faster way to do that, or something to do with the answer once you have
it.

## Asking a question

Type into the question box the same way you'd ask a colleague — full
sentences, not keyword search. For example: *"What did the treating
physician say about causation?"* or *"Is there anything documenting a
prior injury before the incident?"*

While the answer is being prepared, you'll see a brief loading state,
then the answer appears and fills in as it's written — you don't have to
wait for the whole thing before you can start reading.

### Reading the answer

Every answer has two parts, side by side:

- **Answer** — an opening statement, usually followed by a short list of
  supporting points.
- **Sources** — a numbered list of the exact passages the answer drew
  from. Each one shows the document name and page number. Click any
  source to expand it and read the full passage text yourself, or click
  it again to collapse it.

A source card may also show a small badge:

| Badge | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Human-entered note** | This isn't from an ingested document — a staff member entered it directly (see "Adding a note," below). |
| **Approximate date** | The date attached to this source is known approximately, not to the exact day. |
| **Undated** | No date is attached to this source at all. |

If nothing in the case's documents actually answers your question, the
answer will say so plainly rather than guessing — and the source panel
will show that no matching passages were found.

### Asking a follow-up question

You can ask a follow-up without repeating context — for example, after
asking about a diagnosis, you can just ask *"and what about her prior
injuries?"* and it will understand "her" from the question before it.
Each question you ask is added to the conversation as its own entry, so
you can scroll back through everything you've asked and every answer
you got, in order.

When you're done with a case and moving to a genuinely different line of
questioning, click **Start a new conversation** — this clears the thread
so your next question isn't read in the context of the last one.

### Printing a conversation

Click **Print this conversation** to open your browser's print dialog
with every question, answer, and source passage in the thread laid out
on the page — including the full text of every source, not just the
collapsed citation, so a printed copy stands on its own without needing
the screen.

## Asking by voice

If your firm has set up voice, you'll see a microphone icon next to the
question box.

- **Click the microphone** to start recording, then just speak your
  question naturally. Recording stops on its own once you go quiet — you
  don't need to click anything to tell it you're done, though clicking
  the microphone again at any time stops it manually.
- Once it stops, your question is transcribed and **asked automatically**
  — you don't need to click Ask yourself. Talk to it the way you'd talk
  to a voice assistant: ask, and it starts researching right away.
- **The answer is read back to you automatically too** — no need to click
  "Listen to this answer." Ask by voice, and the whole exchange happens
  out loud, start to finish. (Typed questions still show the answer on
  screen only, with **Listen to this answer** available if you want it
  read aloud — asking out loud is what signals you want to hear the
  answer back too.)
- If it mishears something, just say a follow-up out loud to correct it
  — "actually, I meant..." — the same way you'd clarify with a person.
  Every answer is still built only from real cited passages either way,
  so a misheard question can't quietly make up an answer — it just means
  the wrong question got answered, which a quick follow-up fixes.
- **Keyboard shortcut:** press **⌘⇧M** (or **Ctrl+Shift+M** if you're not
  on a Mac) to start or stop recording without touching the mouse — it
  works no matter where your cursor is, including while you're typing.

### Hands-free mode

For a fully hands-free experience, click **Hands-free** in the header.
Once it's on, you'll see a banner across the top confirming it, and you
can start a question just by saying:

> **"Let's do a case review."**

Say that phrase, and it starts recording your actual question
automatically — no click needed at all. Once you stop talking, it
transcribes, asks the question, and reads the answer back — exactly like
the microphone button above — the whole exchange, start to finish, never
needs your hands.

**It keeps the conversation going for follow-up questions, too.** Once
the answer finishes being read back, it starts listening again right
away — no need to repeat "Let's do a case review" for your next
question. Just start talking. This is meant for exactly the way research
actually works: rarely just one question, usually several in a row as
you dig into a case. If you don't have a follow-up, just don't say
anything — it stops listening for one on its own after a few seconds and
goes back to waiting for the wake phrase.

**Or end it yourself by saying:**

> **"That will be all."**

Say that any time you're done with a line of questioning, and it stops
listening for a follow-up right away instead of you having to wait it
out or stay quiet. It won't be treated as a question — nothing gets
asked, nothing shows up in your conversation. Hands-free itself stays on
though, still ready for "Let's do a case review" whenever your next
question comes.

A few things worth knowing about this mode:

- **It only listens for that phrase — it isn't recording your
  conversations.** While hands-free is armed and waiting for the wake
  phrase (that is, between questions — not while it's actively listening
  for your question or a follow-up, when the recording indicator is
  visibly on), short snippets of audio are checked for that specific
  phrase and then discarded; they aren't saved or used for anything
  else. Ordinary conversation in the room doesn't get treated as a
  question — only the phrase itself starts a recording.
- **It's off by default**, and you turn it on explicitly. The banner and
  the header button both stay visible the entire time it's on, so it's
  never ambiguous whether it's armed.
- **It turns itself off after 10 minutes** if it hasn't actually been
  used to start a question in that time — so it doesn't stay armed
  indefinitely in the background.
- **Turn it off any time** — either the **Hands-free** button in the
  header, or the **Turn off** button right in the banner.
- **There's a short delay** between finishing the phrase and recording
  actually starting — a few seconds is normal, not a sign it isn't
  working.
- Consider whether hands-free is appropriate for the room you're in —
  it's well suited to solo case prep, and worth turning off during a
  client meeting or anywhere a live conversation shouldn't be listened
  for.

## Listening to an answer

Any answer to a question you asked out loud is read back to you
automatically once it's ready — see "Asking by voice" above. For a typed
question, click **Listen to this answer** underneath the answer to have
it read aloud. Either way, click the button again (it now says **Stop**)
to stop playback early. While it's being prepared you'll briefly see
"Synthesizing…" — that's normal for longer answers.

## Adding a note

Sometimes you know something relevant to a case that isn't written down
in any document — a phone call with a client, something confirmed
verbally that's worth having on record. Click **+ Add a note** (shown
once a case is selected) to add it directly:

- **Label** — a short description, e.g. "Phone call with client,
  3/10/2024."
- **Note** — what was said or confirmed, in your own words.
- **Date** (optional) — when it happened, if known.
- **Confidence** — only relevant if you gave a date: mark it **Exact**
  if you know the date precisely, or **Approximate** if you're
  estimating.

Once saved, your note becomes a real source — it can be cited in future
answers just like any ingested document, and it's clearly marked
**Human-entered note** wherever it appears so it's never mistaken for
something that came from a filed document.

## Light and dark mode

Click **Dark mode** in the header to switch the whole interface to a
darker color scheme, or click it again (now labeled **Dark mode is on**)
to switch back. Your choice is remembered the next time you open the
tool on the same computer.

## A note on trust

Every factual claim in an answer is required to trace back to a real
passage shown in the source panel — nothing is stated as fact without a
citation you can open and check yourself. If you ever want to verify a
claim before relying on it in a filing or a conversation, click through
to the source and read the original passage in full. That's not an
extra step bolted on for reassurance — it's the same standard the answer
itself was held to when it was written.

## Privacy

Everything you ask, and everything the case documents contain, is
processed entirely on the machine this tool runs on, inside your firm's
own office. Nothing about a case — the documents, your questions, the
answers — is sent to any outside service to be answered. The same is
true of voice: if you use the microphone or have an answer read aloud,
that audio is also handled entirely on the same local machine.

## Getting help

If something in the tool isn't working the way this guide describes, or
an answer seems wrong, contact whoever manages this system at your firm.
