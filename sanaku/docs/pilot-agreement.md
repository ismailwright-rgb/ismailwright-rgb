### 3. What counts as a "qualified lead"

A qualified lead is a person who contacted your business and whom our system captured, where **all** of the following are true:

- They provided a working phone number or email address
- They expressed interest in [YOUR SERVICE — e.g. plumbing service, a consultation]
- They are not an existing scheduled customer, a vendor, a spam or robocall, or a wrong number

Every captured lead is logged with a timestamp and is visible to you on request. **You are only billed for leads our system captured** — not for calls your team answered directly. If you believe a lead was miscounted, tell us within 15 days and we'll remove it, no argument.

### 4. Term and cancellation

This agreement runs month to month. Either of us may cancel with **[14/30] days' written notice** (email is fine). On cancellation:

- We stop billing at the end of the notice period
- We hand over your captured lead data in a spreadsheet within 7 days
- We shut off the workflows; we do not delete your data for 30 days in case you change your mind

There is no long-term commitment and no cancellation penalty.

### 5. What we need from you

- Access to point your business phone number or web form at our system (we'll walk you through it)
- Your logo, business name, and preferred message wording for customer-facing texts
- A phone number or email where urgent leads should be escalated
- Approval of the message templates before anything goes live

### 6. Messaging compliance

Messages are sent only to people who contacted **your** business first. Every message identifies your business and honors opt-out requests ("STOP") immediately and permanently. We register the sending number as required by carrier rules. You agree not to ask us to send messages to purchased lists or to people who have not contacted you.

### 7. Data

Your customers' contact information belongs to you. We store it to operate the service, we don't sell or share it, and we delete it on request. We'll honor any customer's deletion or opt-out request promptly.

### 8. Limits

We'll operate the workflows with reasonable care but we don't guarantee a specific number of leads, bookings, or sales. Our total liability in any month is limited to the fees you paid us that month. Neither of us is liable to the other for indirect or consequential damages.

## Before you send it — vertical notes

**Home services (plumbing / HVAC / electrical)** — use as written. This is the
only vertical where a revenue-share structure is also acceptable if you'd
rather do that; add it as a percentage line in section 2.

**Medical / dental / med spa** — **delete the per-lead row from section 2 and
use a flat monthly fee only.** Per-patient and percentage arrangements can
implicate federal anti-kickback rules and state fee-splitting law. Don't get
creative here; a flat fee is clean and nobody's compliance officer objects.

**Personal injury law** — **never** propose a percentage of fees or case
recoveries; most states bar lawyers from splitting fees with non-lawyers, and
a firm that agrees without hesitation is one to be wary of. Flat monthly, or
retainer plus a fee per **lead delivered** (not per case signed), both work
and are the standard structures firms already approve.

## Pricing quick reference

| Vertical | Setup | Monthly | Per lead |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home services | per service | $500–$1,000 | $50 (cap it) |
| Dental / med spa | per service | $500–$1,200 | — flat only |
| PI law | per service | $1,000–$2,500 | $100 per lead delivered, or flat |

**Why keep the setup fee:** free pilots attract people who were never going to
buy. Charging setup up front filters tire-kickers, pays for your build time, and
gets cash in on day one. It is the service's own setup fee — see the services
sheet for the figure — not a flat number, because a $1,500 build and a $250 one
do not cost the same to stand up. De-risk the *retainer* instead — that's what the free pilot window in
section 2 is for.
